Silk begins with a cocoon that is handled well.

We are students building three low-cost tools for the pre-cocoon stage of sericulture: where farmers lose the most time, labour and money.

Meet the tools Read the problem

Theme: Empowering Hands

The problem sits between the mountage and the market

After silkworms spin, cocoons pass through three manual steps. Every step is slow, and a mistake in one is carried into the next.

Hired harvesting labour alone costs a farmer about ₹30,000 a year. Uneven cocoons make machine handling hard, and sorting by eye is inconsistent.

Aakar

Collapsible plastic mountage

Farmers know the hexagonal chandrika, so we kept its form and redesigned how it is built. Flat panels with honeycomb cuts are joined by hinges. Set on a ridged frame, they take a triangular shape; lifted off, they fold flat.

  • Familiar hexagonal cells
  • Folds flat for storage and transport
  • Aims for uniform cocoon size and shape

Nivara

Cocoon harvester

The plastic chandrika rests on a frame with triangular ridges, so it folds evenly. A matching panel presses it down like a scanner lid. A scraper plate with the same triangles is pushed across by hand, lifting every cocoon off into a tray.

  • Hand operated and low cost
  • Scraper matches each fold
  • Our target: almost 5x harvesting efficiency

Vidura

Colour-sensor cocoon sorter

Cocoons leave a hopper one at a time through an air lock gate. TCS34725 colour sensors and an HX711 load cell read colour and weight, and an ESP32 sends each cocoon to the good or bad block.

  • Sensor based, no cameras needed
  • Reads colour and weight
  • Built to fit small farms and budgets

All three tools are in the prototyping phase and are still being developed.

Built with people who work with silk

We visited KSSRDI, Bangalore and CSTRI, Mysore, and learned from Dr Sunil Rathode, Scientist B at the Central Silk Board, Bangalore.

Farmers in Karnataka and Maharashtra showed us where the work hurts. Their feedback decided what we kept and what we changed, including a new design for Nivara.
