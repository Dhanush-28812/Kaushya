Silk starts in careful hands.

Team Kaushya builds three low-cost tools for the first days of silk, where farmers lose the most time and money.

Meet the toolsWhy we built them

Wisdom World School, Wakad  |  Empowering Hands

Hired harvesting alone costs a farmer about ₹30,000 every year.

After the worms spin, cocoons go through three manual steps. Each is slow, and a mistake in one is carried into the next. We looked at every step with farmers and scientists, and built one tool for each.

  • Mounting
  • Harvesting
  • Sorting

Stage 1: Mounting

Aakar

Collapsible plastic mountage

Farmers trust the hexagonal chandrika, so we kept its form and rebuilt how it is made. Flat panels with honeycomb cuts are joined by hinges. On a ridged frame they take a triangular shape; lifted off, they fold flat.

  • Familiar hexagonal cells
  • Folds flat to store and carry
  • Aims for uniform cocoon size and shape

Stage 2: Harvesting

Nivara

Cocoon harvester

The chandrika rests on a ridged frame so it folds evenly. A matching panel presses it down like a scanner lid, then a scraper with the same triangles is pushed across by hand, lifting every cocoon into a tray.

  • Hand operated and low cost
  • Scraper matches each fold
  • Our target: almost 5x harvesting efficiency

Stage 3: Sorting

Vidura

Colour-sensor cocoon sorter

Cocoons leave a hopper one at a time through an air lock gate. TCS34725 colour sensors and an HX711 load cell read colour and weight, and an ESP32 sends each cocoon to the good or bad block.

  • Sensor based, no cameras needed
  • Reads colour and weight
  • Built to fit small farms and budgets

All three tools are in the prototyping phase and are still being developed.
